# History: events and periods

## Mesopotamia (first cities and writing)
Mesopotamia was the region between the Tigris and Euphrates rivers, in present-day Iraq. From about 3500 BCE its cities such as Uruk and Ur produced the first writing, the first wheeled vehicles and some of the earliest law codes.

## Sumer (first civilisation)
Sumer was the southern part of Mesopotamia, where the world's first cities grew from about 4000 BCE. The Sumerians wrote in cuneiform on clay tablets and built large stepped temples called ziggurats.

## Ancient Egypt (civilisation of the Nile)
Ancient Egypt was a civilisation along the Nile that lasted from about 3100 BCE to 30 BCE. It is known for the pharaohs, the pyramids at Giza, hieroglyphic writing and a calendar based on the river's yearly flood.

## Ancient Greece (city-states and democracy)
Ancient Greece was the civilisation of the Greek city-states, from about 800 BCE to the Roman conquest in 146 BCE. Athens developed an early form of democracy, and Greek thinkers laid foundations in philosophy, history, drama and geometry.

## Ancient Rome (republic and empire)
Ancient Rome began as a city-state and became a republic, then an empire that at its height ringed the Mediterranean. Roman law, Latin, roads and engineering shaped later Europe, and the western empire ended in 476 CE.

## Persian Empire (Achaemenids)
The Persian Empire founded by Cyrus the Great around 550 BCE became the largest empire the world had then seen, stretching from Egypt to the Indus. It was run with provinces called satrapies and ended with Alexander's conquest in 330 BCE.

## Qin dynasty (first emperor of China)
The Qin dynasty ruled China from 221 to 206 BCE and first united the country under one emperor, Qin Shi Huang. It standardised writing, weights, measures and money, and began the Great Wall; the emperor's tomb holds the terracotta army.

## Han dynasty (China's long golden age)
The Han dynasty ruled China from 206 BCE to 220 CE. It expanded trade along the Silk Road, promoted Confucian ideas in government and is the period from which the Chinese majority takes its name.

## Maya civilisation (Mesoamerica)
The Maya civilisation flourished in what is now southern Mexico, Guatemala, Belize and Honduras, with its peak between about 250 and 900 CE. The Maya built stone cities with stepped pyramids, kept a precise calendar and developed a full writing system.

## Inca Empire (Andes)
The Inca Empire was the largest empire of the pre-Columbian Americas, centred in the Andes at Cusco in present-day Peru. From about 1438 to the Spanish conquest in the 1530s it ran a road network and stored goods in warehouses, without a written script.

## Aztec Empire (Mexico)
The Aztec Empire was centred on the city of Tenochtitlan, on the site of modern Mexico City, from 1428 until the Spanish conquest in 1521. The Aztecs built a large city on an island in a lake and ran an empire of tribute-paying cities.

## Fall of Rome (476 CE)
In 476 CE the German leader Odoacer deposed Romulus Augustulus, the last emperor of the western Roman Empire. Historians usually take this as the end of the western empire, though the eastern empire continued for nearly a thousand years.

## Byzantine Empire (330-1453)
The Byzantine Empire was the eastern Roman Empire, ruled from Constantinople. It lasted from 330 CE until the Ottoman conquest of the city in 1453, and it preserved Greek learning and produced the legal code of Justinian.

## Fall of Constantinople (1453)
In 1453 the Ottoman army under Sultan Mehmed II captured Constantinople after a siege. The city became the Ottoman capital, and the event is often taken as the end of the Middle Ages in Europe.

## Islamic Golden Age (about 800-1250)
The Islamic Golden Age was a period of science, medicine, mathematics and philosophy in the Islamic world. Scholars in Baghdad, Córdoba and Cairo translated Greek works and made advances in algebra, optics and hospitals.

## Mongol Empire (1206-1368)
The Mongol Empire was founded by Genghis Khan in 1206 and became the largest land empire in history. It stretched from China to eastern Europe and, by linking trade routes, made travel between Asia and Europe easier.

## Black Death (1347-1351)
The Black Death was a pandemic of plague that reached Europe in 1347 and killed a large share of its people within a few years. It spread along trade routes, and the deaths changed wages, land use and society.

## Renaissance (about 1400-1600)
The Renaissance was a period of renewed interest in classical learning and art, beginning in Italy around 1400. It produced artists such as Leonardo da Vinci and Michelangelo and new ways of studying nature and the human body.

## Printing press (about 1450)
Around 1450 Johannes Gutenberg, working in Mainz in Germany, developed a printing press with movable metal type. Printing spread across Europe within decades, making books far cheaper and helping ideas travel faster.

## Christopher Columbus and 1492
In 1492 Christopher Columbus, sailing for Spain, crossed the Atlantic and reached islands in the Caribbean. His voyages began sustained contact between Europe and the Americas, along with conquest and the spread of disease.

## Reformation (1517)
The Reformation began in 1517 when Martin Luther in Wittenberg published his Ninety-five Theses criticising the sale of indulgences. It split western Christianity into Catholic and Protestant churches and reshaped European politics.

## Ottoman Empire (1299-1922)
The Ottoman Empire began around 1299 in Anatolia and grew into an empire covering the Balkans, the Middle East and North Africa. It was ruled from Constantinople from 1453 and ended in 1922 after its defeat in World War I.

## Scientific Revolution (about 1550-1700)
The Scientific Revolution was a period when understanding of nature shifted to observation and mathematics. Copernicus placed the sun at the centre of the solar system, Galileo and Kepler confirmed it, and Newton set out the laws of motion and gravity in 1687.

## Industrial Revolution (about 1760-1900)
The Industrial Revolution began in Britain in the late 1700s, when steam power and machines moved production into factories. Railways, iron ships and mass production spread to other countries and changed how people worked and lived.

## American Revolution and 1776
The American Revolution was the war in which the thirteen British colonies in North America became independent. The Declaration of Independence was adopted on 4 July 1776, and the war ended with the Treaty of Paris in 1783.

## United States independence (4 July 1776)
The thirteen British colonies in North America declared independence on 4 July 1776, which the United States now marks as its national day. The declaration set out the idea that governments derive their authority from the people they govern.

## French Revolution (1789)
The French Revolution began in 1789 when the king called the Estates-General and Parisians stormed the Bastille on 14 July. It ended the monarchy, produced the Declaration of the Rights of Man and led to years of war in Europe.

## Napoleon (Emperor 1804-1815)
Napoleon Bonaparte took power in France in 1799 and crowned himself emperor in 1804. His armies dominated Europe for a decade until the failed invasion of Russia in 1812 and final defeat at Waterloo in 1815; his legal code still shapes law in many countries.

## Abolition of slavery (when it ended in different countries)
The slave trade and then slavery itself were abolished by different countries at different times. Britain ended its slave trade in 1807 and slavery in most of its empire in 1833, France abolished slavery in its colonies in 1848, the United States in 1865 and Brazil in 1888.

## World War I (1914-1918)
World War I was fought from 1914 to 1918, mainly in Europe. It began after the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand in Sarajevo in June 1914, and the fighting on the western front was largely in trenches; the armistice came on 11 November 1918.

## Russian Revolution (1917)
The Russian Revolution took place in 1917 in two stages: the February revolution ended the tsar's rule and the October revolution brought the Bolsheviks under Lenin to power. It led to a civil war and the founding of the Soviet Union in 1922.

## Republic of Türkiye founded (1923)
The Republic of Türkiye was founded on 29 October 1923, with Mustafa Kemal Atatürk as its first president. It replaced the Ottoman state and brought in a series of reforms in law, education and the alphabet.

## Treaty of Lausanne (1923)
The Treaty of Lausanne, signed in 1923, settled the borders of the new Republic of Türkiye after the Ottoman defeat. It replaced the harsher Treaty of Sèvres and is still the basis of Türkiye's western and southern borders.

## Great Depression (from 1929)
The Great Depression began with the crash of the New York stock market in October 1929. Banks failed, world trade fell sharply and millions lost work through the 1930s, and governments responded with new economic policies.

## World War II (1939-1945)
World War II was fought from 1939 to 1945 and was the deadliest war in history. It began with the German invasion of Poland in September 1939, spread to Europe, Africa, Asia and the Pacific, and ended in Europe in May 1945 and in the Pacific in September 1945.

## Holocaust (1941-1945)
The Holocaust was the killing of about six million Jews and millions of others by Nazi Germany and its collaborators. The systematic persecution was organised from the early 1940s and is remembered in museums and memorials around the world.

## Hiroshima and Nagasaki (August 1945)
In August 1945 the United States dropped atomic bombs on the Japanese cities of Hiroshima and Nagasaki. Japan announced its surrender days later, ending World War II in the Pacific.

## United Nations founded (1945)
The United Nations was founded in 1945, when its charter was signed in San Francisco and came into force on 24 October. It replaced the League of Nations and works on peace, development and human rights.

## Universal Declaration of Human Rights (1948)
The Universal Declaration of Human Rights was adopted by the United Nations in Paris on 10 December 1948. It lists basic rights for all people and is one of the most translated documents in the world.

## Indian independence (1947)
India became independent from Britain on 15 August 1947, after a long movement led by Mohandas Gandhi and others. The country was divided at the same time into India and Pakistan, and the partition caused huge migrations and violence.

## Cold War (about 1947-1991)
The Cold War was a long rivalry between the United States and its allies and the Soviet Union and its allies. It was fought through arms races, alliances, espionage, propaganda and wars in other countries, without direct war between the two superpowers.

## NATO founded (1949)
NATO, the North Atlantic Treaty Organization, was founded in 1949 by the United States, Canada and western European countries. It is a defence alliance whose members agree that an attack on one is an attack on all.

## Warsaw Pact (1955-1991)
The Warsaw Pact was a defence alliance of the Soviet Union and its eastern European allies, formed in 1955. It was dissolved in 1991 after the end of the Cold War.

## First satellite (Sputnik, 1957)
The Soviet Union launched Sputnik 1, the first artificial satellite, on 4 October 1957. It circled the Earth for three months and began the space age and the space race between the Soviet Union and the United States.

## First human in space (1961)
Yuri Gagarin of the Soviet Union became the first human in space on 12 April 1961, orbiting the Earth once in Vostok 1. The flight lasted 108 minutes and was a major event of the space race.

## First Moon landing (1969)
On 20 July 1969 the American Apollo 11 mission landed on the Moon. Neil Armstrong, the first person to walk on the Moon, and Buzz Aldrin walked on its surface while Michael Collins stayed in orbit, and the landing was watched on television around the world.

## Fall of the Berlin Wall (1989)
The Berlin Wall, which had divided the city since 1961, was opened on 9 November 1989. Crowds crossed and began to take the wall down, and the event became the symbol of the end of the division of Europe.

## Dissolution of the Soviet Union (1991)
The Soviet Union was dissolved in December 1991, when its republics became independent states. Russia took the Soviet Union's seat at the United Nations, and fifteen new countries appeared.

## World Wide Web (1989-1991)
Tim Berners-Lee proposed the World Wide Web at CERN in 1989, and the first website and web browser followed in 1990 and 1991. CERN put the technology in the public domain in 1993, and the web made the internet usable for ordinary people.

## Euro (1999 and 2002)
The euro became the currency of eleven European Union countries on 1 January 1999, at first only for accounting. Coins and notes entered daily use on 1 January 2002, and the euro is now used by a large group of European countries.

## Magna Carta (1215)
Magna Carta was a charter agreed by King John of England in 1215. It set limits on the power of the king and is often cited as an early step in the idea of rule by law.

## Hundred Years' War (1337-1453)
The Hundred Years' War was a series of conflicts between England and France from 1337 to 1453. It ended with France holding most of the disputed lands, and it helped shape a sense of national identity in both countries.

## Spanish conquest of the Americas (16th century)
Spain conquered the Aztec Empire in 1521 and the Inca Empire in the 1530s. Spanish rule spread over much of the Americas, and the period saw the destruction of native states and a huge fall in native populations from war and disease.

## Magellan and the first voyage around the world (1519-1522)
Ferdinand Magellan led a Spanish expedition that set out in 1519 to reach Asia by sailing west. Magellan died in the Philippines, but one ship under Juan Sebastián Elcano returned to Spain in 1522, completing the first voyage around the world.

## Thirty Years' War (1618-1648)
The Thirty Years' War was fought mainly in central Europe from 1618 to 1648, starting as a religious conflict and becoming a wider struggle for power. The peace of Westphalia in 1648 ended it and is often cited as the origin of the modern idea of state sovereignty.

## English Civil War (1642-1651)
The English Civil War was fought between supporters of the king and supporters of Parliament from 1642. King Charles I was executed in 1649 and a republic called the Commonwealth was declared under Oliver Cromwell.

## Glorious Revolution (1688)
In 1688 the English king James II was replaced by William and Mary in a change of power with little fighting. It led to a Bill of Rights in 1689 and to a system in which Parliament, not the monarch alone, held the main power.

## American Civil War (1861-1865)
The American Civil War was fought from 1861 to 1865 between the northern states and the southern states that left the Union. It ended with the defeat of the South and the abolition of slavery across the United States.

## Unification of Italy (1861)
Italy became a single kingdom in 1861, after a movement led by figures including Giuseppe Garibaldi and Camillo di Cavour. Rome joined the kingdom in 1870, completing unification.

## Unification of Germany (1871)
Germany became a single empire in 1871, after Prussia under Otto von Bismarck won wars against Denmark, Austria and France. The empire was proclaimed at Versailles in January 1871 and lasted until 1918.

## Meiji Restoration (1868)
The Meiji Restoration was a change of power in Japan in 1868, when rule by the shogun ended and the emperor was restored. Japan then industrialised and modernised its army and institutions within a few decades.

## Scramble for Africa (1880s-1900s)
The scramble for Africa was the rapid takeover of most of Africa by European powers from the 1880s to about 1914. At the Berlin Conference of 1884-1885 the powers agreed rules for the partition, and the colonial borders shaped later independent states.

## Suez Canal opened (1869)
The Suez Canal opened in 1869, joining the Mediterranean Sea to the Red Sea through Egypt. It shortened the sea route between Europe and Asia by thousands of kilometres, and it is still one of the world's busiest waterways.

## Panama Canal opened (1914)
The Panama Canal opened to ships in August 1914, cutting across the isthmus of Panama. It allows ships to pass between the Atlantic and Pacific without sailing around South America, and uses locks to lift them over the land.

## Transcontinental railroad (United States, 1869)
The first transcontinental railroad across the United States was completed on 10 May 1869, joining the Union Pacific and Central Pacific lines in Utah. It cut a journey of months to about a week.

## Sinking of the Titanic (1912)
The passenger liner Titanic struck an iceberg and sank on its first voyage in April 1912, with heavy loss of life. The disaster led to new international rules on lifeboats and radio watches at sea.

## League of Nations (1920-1946)
The League of Nations was founded in 1920 as the first worldwide organisation for keeping peace. It failed to prevent the conflicts of the 1930s, and it was replaced by the United Nations in 1946.

## Spanish Civil War (1936-1939)
The Spanish Civil War was fought from 1936 to 1939 between the Republican government and nationalist forces under General Franco. It ended with Franco's victory and a dictatorship that lasted until 1975.

## Berlin Blockade and airlift (1948-1949)
In 1948 the Soviet Union blocked road and rail links to West Berlin, and the western powers supplied the city by air for nearly a year. The blockade was lifted in May 1949, and Germany was soon divided into two states.

## Korean War (1950-1953)
The Korean War was fought from 1950 to 1953 between North Korea and China on one side and South Korea and United Nations forces on the other. It ended with an armistice that has never been replaced by a peace treaty.

## Cuban Missile Crisis (1962)
The Cuban Missile Crisis of October 1962 was a confrontation between the United States and the Soviet Union over Soviet missiles placed in Cuba. It ended after the Soviet Union withdrew the missiles, and it led to a direct telephone line between the two capitals.

## Civil rights movement (United States, 1950s-1960s)
The civil rights movement in the United States worked to end racial segregation and discrimination, especially in the South. It used protests, court cases and boycotts, and won laws in 1964 and 1965; Martin Luther King Jr led much of it.

## Berlin Wall built (1961)
The Berlin Wall was built in August 1961 by East Germany to stop people crossing from East to West Berlin. It divided families and became a symbol of the Cold War until it was opened in 1989.

## Vietnam War (about 1955-1975)
The Vietnam War was fought between North Vietnam and the Viet Cong against South Vietnam and the United States. It ended in 1975 with the fall of Saigon and the reunification of Vietnam under one government.

## Apollo programme (1961-1972)
The Apollo programme was the United States effort to land people on the Moon, announced by President Kennedy in 1961. Six missions landed on the Moon between 1969 and 1972, and the programme is remembered for the first Moon landing.

## Chernobyl disaster (1986)
The nuclear power station at Chernobyl in Ukraine suffered a major accident on 26 April 1986. A reactor exploded and released radiation over a wide area, and the site is still sealed off for safety.

## End of apartheid (1990-1994)
Apartheid, the system of racial separation in South Africa, began to be dismantled in 1990 when Nelson Mandela was released from prison. The first election open to all races was held in 1994, and Mandela became president.

## Rwandan genocide (1994)
In 1994, over about one hundred days, around half a million people were killed in Rwanda, mainly Tutsi by Hutu militias. The killings followed the shooting down of the president's plane, and the event led to international tribunals.

## Genome project (Human Genome Project, 1990-2003)
The Human Genome Project was an international effort begun in 1990 to map and read the human genetic code. A working draft was published in 2000 and the map was completed in 2003.

## Luna 2 and the far side of the Moon (1959)
In 1959 the Soviet spacecraft Luna 2 became the first object from Earth to reach the Moon, hitting its surface. Later that year Luna 3 sent back the first photographs of the Moon's far side, which is never seen from Earth.

## Viking landers on Mars (1976)
In 1976 two American Viking spacecraft landed on Mars and sent back the first pictures from its surface. They tested the soil for signs of life and found none conclusive, and they began decades of robot exploration of the planet.

## Sanitation and sewer systems (19th century)
In the 19th century cities built sewer systems and supplied clean water, beginning with works such as London's sewers in the 1860s. Together with sewage treatment, these changes greatly reduced deaths from diseases spread by dirty water.

## Vaccination (from 1796)
In 1796 Edward Jenner showed that cowpox could protect people against smallpox. Vaccination became a routine protection against many diseases and led eventually to the worldwide eradication of smallpox, declared in 1980.
