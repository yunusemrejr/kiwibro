# Chemistry reference

## Atom
An atom is the smallest unit of an element that still has the properties of that element. It has a tiny dense nucleus of protons and neutrons, surrounded by electrons. Atoms are the building blocks of all matter.

## Proton
A proton is a particle found in the nucleus of every atom. It carries a positive electric charge and its number in the nucleus is the atomic number, which decides which element the atom is.

## Neutron
A neutron is a particle in the atomic nucleus with no electric charge and slightly more mass than a proton. Neutrons hold the nucleus together and add to the atom's mass. Atoms of the same element can have different numbers of them.

## Electron
An electron is a very light particle with a negative charge that surrounds the atomic nucleus. Electrons are involved in chemical bonding and carry electric current in wires. Their arrangement explains most of an element's chemistry.

## Atomic number
The atomic number of an element is the number of protons in the nucleus of one of its atoms. It is the number that places the element in the periodic table. Every atom of the same element has the same atomic number.

## Ion
An ion is an atom or group of atoms with an electric charge because it has gained or lost electrons. Losing electrons gives a positive ion (cation); gaining electrons gives a negative one (anion). Sodium chloride is held together by ions.

## Isotope
Isotopes are atoms of the same element with the same number of protons but different numbers of neutrons, so they have different masses. For example carbon-12 and carbon-14 are isotopes of carbon. Some isotopes are radioactive.

## Molecule
A molecule is a group of atoms joined by covalent bonds, and it is the smallest unit of a substance that keeps its chemical identity. Water is a molecule of two hydrogen atoms and one oxygen atom. Some elements exist as molecules, such as O2.

## Compound
A compound is a substance made of atoms of two or more different elements chemically joined in fixed proportions. Water and carbon dioxide are compounds. A compound usually has properties unlike those of the elements it contains.

## Mixture
A mixture contains two or more substances that are not chemically joined, so each keeps its own properties. Salt and sand stirred together, or air, are mixtures. Mixtures can be separated by physical methods such as filtering or distillation.

## Chemical element
A chemical element is a pure substance made of only one kind of atom. There are 118 known elements, each with its own atomic number. Elements are the basic materials from which all substances are built.

## Periodic table
The periodic table arranges the chemical elements in order of atomic number, in rows and columns. Elements in the same column have similar chemical properties. It was first put together by Dmitri Mendeleev in the 19th century.

## Groups of the periodic table (columns)
A group is a vertical column of the periodic table. Elements in the same group have the same number of electrons in their outer shell, which gives them similar chemical behaviour. Group 1 contains the alkali metals and group 18 the noble gases.

## Periods of the periodic table (rows)
A period is a horizontal row of the periodic table. Moving across a period, atoms gain protons and outer electrons and the elements change from metals through metalloids to nonmetals. There are seven periods.

## Chemical bond
A chemical bond is the force that holds atoms together in compounds. The main types are ionic bonds, covalent bonds and metallic bonds. Bonding happens because atoms become more stable when their outer electron arrangements change.

## Ionic bond
An ionic bond forms when one atom gives electrons to another, creating oppositely charged ions that attract each other. Ionic compounds have high melting points and often dissolve in water. Table salt is an ionic compound.

## Covalent bond
A covalent bond forms when two atoms share a pair of electrons. It is common between nonmetals and holds molecules such as water and oxygen together. Covalent bonds are usually strong, and many covalent substances have low melting points.

## Metallic bond
A metallic bond forms when metal atoms share their outer electrons in a sea of free electrons. This explains why metals conduct electricity and heat, and why they can be bent without breaking. The shared electrons also give metals their shine.

## Acid
An acid is a substance that releases hydrogen ions (H+) when dissolved in water. Acids turn blue litmus paper red and taste sour, as lemon juice does. Common acids include hydrochloric acid and sulfuric acid.

## Base
A base is a substance that neutralises an acid, often by accepting hydrogen ions. A soluble base such as sodium hydroxide is called an alkali. Bases turn red litmus paper blue and feel slippery.

## pH scale
The pH scale measures how acidic or alkaline a solution is, from 0 to 14. A pH of 7 is neutral, below 7 is acidic and above 7 is alkaline. Each step of one unit means a tenfold change in acidity.

## Neutralisation
Neutralisation is the reaction between an acid and a base, which produces a salt and water. Hydrochloric acid and sodium hydroxide give sodium chloride and water. The pH of the solution moves towards neutral.

## Water (H2O)
Water is a compound of two hydrogen atoms and one oxygen atom, formula H2O. It is a liquid at room temperature with a boiling point of 100 degrees Celsius at sea level. Water dissolves more substances than most liquids, which is why it is called the universal solvent.

## Carbon dioxide (CO2)
Carbon dioxide is a compound of one carbon atom and two oxygen atoms, formula CO2. It is a colourless gas made by burning carbon compounds and by respiration, and plants use it in photosynthesis. It is a greenhouse gas.

## Sodium chloride (NaCl)
Sodium chloride, formula NaCl, is common table salt and the compound of sodium and chlorine. It forms a regular crystal lattice of positive sodium and negative chloride ions. It is used to season and preserve food and is essential in the body.

## Methane (CH4)
Methane is a compound of one carbon atom and four hydrogen atoms, formula CH4. It is the simplest hydrocarbon and the main gas in natural gas. It burns with a pale blue flame to give carbon dioxide and water.

## Ammonia (NH3)
Ammonia is a compound of one nitrogen atom and three hydrogen atoms, formula NH3. It is a colourless gas with a sharp smell, and it dissolves very well in water. It is used to make fertilisers.

## Glucose (C6H12O6)
Glucose is a sugar with formula C6H12O6, made by plants in photosynthesis. It is the main fuel that living cells break down for energy. It is also known as blood sugar.

## Oxygen (O2)
Oxygen gas has the formula O2, two oxygen atoms joined by a covalent bond. It makes up about 21% of the air and is used in respiration and combustion. Oxygen also supports combustion, so a glowing splint relights in it.

## Ozone (O3)
Ozone is a form of oxygen with three atoms per molecule, formula O3. It is a pale blue gas with a sharp smell and is a much stronger oxidising agent than ordinary oxygen. The ozone layer high in the atmosphere shields the Earth from harmful ultraviolet light.

## Hydrogen peroxide (H2O2)
Hydrogen peroxide has the formula H2O2, two hydrogen atoms joined to two oxygen atoms. It is a liquid that breaks down into water and oxygen. A dilute solution is used as a disinfectant and a bleach.

## Sulfuric acid (H2SO4)
Sulfuric acid has the formula H2SO4 and is a strong acid. It is one of the most widely produced industrial chemicals, used in fertilisers, batteries and metal processing. It is corrosive and must be handled with care.

## Ethanol (C2H5OH)
Ethanol is an alcohol with formula C2H5OH. It is the alcohol found in alcoholic drinks and is made by fermenting sugars. It is also used as a fuel and as a solvent.

## Sodium bicarbonate (NaHCO3)
Sodium bicarbonate, formula NaHCO3, is commonly called baking soda. It is a mild base that reacts with acids to release carbon dioxide, which makes cakes rise. It is also used as an antacid and a cleaning agent.

## Mole (unit of amount)
A mole is the amount of a substance that contains 6.022 * 10^23 particles, a number called the Avogadro constant. The mole lets chemists count atoms by weighing. One mole of a substance has a mass in grams equal to its molar mass.

## Molar mass
The molar mass is the mass of one mole of a substance, measured in grams per mole. It is found by adding the atomic masses of all the atoms in the formula. Water, H2O, has a molar mass of about 18 grams per mole.

## Oxidation
Oxidation is the loss of electrons by a substance during a chemical reaction. It often involves gaining oxygen or losing hydrogen, as when iron rusts. Oxidation is always accompanied by reduction.

## Reduction
Reduction is the gain of electrons by a substance during a chemical reaction. It often involves losing oxygen or gaining hydrogen, as when an oxide is turned back into a metal. A memory aid is that reduction is the opposite of oxidation.

## Combustion
Combustion is a chemical reaction in which a fuel reacts with oxygen and releases energy as heat and light. Burning a hydrocarbon such as methane produces carbon dioxide and water. Complete combustion needs plenty of oxygen; without it, carbon monoxide can form.

## Photosynthesis equation
Photosynthesis is how plants make glucose from carbon dioxide and water using light energy: 6 CO2 + 6 H2O -> C6H12O6 + 6 O2. It takes place mainly in the chloroplasts of leaves. It releases oxygen into the air.

## Respiration equation
Cellular respiration releases energy from glucose: C6H12O6 + 6 O2 -> 6 CO2 + 6 H2O + energy. It happens in cells, mainly in the mitochondria. It is the reverse of photosynthesis in terms of the substances involved.

## Catalyst
A catalyst is a substance that speeds up a chemical reaction without being used up itself. It works by providing a route with a lower activation energy. Enzymes are natural catalysts, and catalytic converters use metals to clean car exhaust.

## Solution
A solution is a mixture in which a solute is dissolved evenly in a solvent, so the result is the same throughout. Salt water is a solution. Solutions cannot be separated by filtering.

## Solvent
A solvent is the liquid in which a solute dissolves to make a solution. Water is the most common solvent. The solvent is the substance present in the larger amount.

## Solute
A solute is the substance that dissolves in a solvent to form a solution. In salt water, the salt is the solute. The solute is present in the smaller amount.

## Concentration
Concentration is the amount of solute in a given amount of solution. It is measured in grams per cubic decimetre or moles per cubic decimetre. A more concentrated solution has more solute in the same volume.

## Boiling point of water
At sea level, where air pressure is standard, water boils at 100 degrees Celsius (212 degrees Fahrenheit). At higher altitudes the pressure is lower, so water boils at a lower temperature. Additives such as salt raise the boiling point slightly.

## Freezing point of water
Water freezes at 0 degrees Celsius (32 degrees Fahrenheit) at sea level. Unlike most substances, water expands when it freezes, which is why ice floats. Salt lowers the freezing point, which is why it is spread on icy roads.

## States of matter and phase changes
The three common states of matter are solid, liquid and gas. Heating a solid melts it to a liquid, and heating a liquid boils it to a gas; cooling reverses the changes. Melting, freezing, boiling, condensation and sublimation are the main phase changes.

## Alloy
An alloy is a mixture of a metal with one or more other elements, made by melting them together. Alloys are usually stronger or more resistant to corrosion than the pure metal. Steel, bronze and brass are alloys.

## Steel
Steel is an alloy of iron with a small amount of carbon, usually under 2%. Adding other metals such as chromium produces stainless steel, which resists rust. Steel is the most widely used metal material.

## Bronze
Bronze is an alloy of copper and tin. It is harder than pure copper and was used to make tools, weapons and statues in ancient times. The Bronze Age is named after it.

## Brass
Brass is an alloy of copper and zinc. It is yellow, resists corrosion and is easy to shape, so it is used for fittings, instruments and decorative objects.

## Composition of air
Dry air near the ground is about 78% nitrogen and 21% oxygen by volume, with about 0.9% argon and about 0.04% carbon dioxide. There are also tiny amounts of other gases. The air usually contains some water vapour as well.

## Chemical reaction
A chemical reaction is a process in which substances change into new substances with different properties. Bonds break and new ones form, and atoms are rearranged but not lost. Signs of a reaction include colour change, gas, heat and light.

## Exothermic and endothermic reactions
An exothermic reaction releases energy to the surroundings, usually as heat, such as burning fuel. An endothermic reaction takes energy in, such as the reaction in a cold pack. Both types still need some energy to get started.

## Chemical formula
A chemical formula shows the elements in a compound and how many atoms of each are present, such as H2O for water. Subscripts count the atoms of the element just before them. A formula without a number means one atom.

## Solubility
Solubility is the amount of a solute that can dissolve in a given amount of solvent at a given temperature. It is often measured in grams per 100 grams of water. Most solids dissolve better in warm water, while gases dissolve better in cold water.

## Electrolysis
Electrolysis uses an electric current to drive a chemical reaction that would not happen on its own, decomposing a compound into its elements. It breaks molten or dissolved compounds into parts at the electrodes. It is used to extract metals such as aluminium and to split water into hydrogen and oxygen.

## Distillation
Distillation separates liquids by boiling them and condensing the vapour. The liquid with the lower boiling point comes off first. It is used to purify water and to separate crude oil into fractions.

## Hydrocarbon
A hydrocarbon is a compound made only of carbon and hydrogen, such as methane and petrol components. Most fuels are hydrocarbons. Burning them releases energy and produces carbon dioxide and water.

## Valence electrons
Valence electrons are the electrons in the outermost shell of an atom. They are the ones involved in forming chemical bonds. An atom with a full outer shell, such as a noble gas, is chemically stable.
