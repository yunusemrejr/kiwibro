# Biology reference

## Cell
A cell is the smallest unit of life, and all living things are made of one or more cells. It is surrounded by a membrane and contains the chemicals and structures needed for life. Most cells are far too small to see without a microscope.

## Cell theory
Cell theory states that all living things are made of cells, that the cell is the basic unit of life, and that new cells come from existing cells. It is one of the foundations of biology. All three parts are supported by observation.

## Prokaryotes and eukaryotes
Prokaryotes are cells with no nucleus or membrane-bound organelles; bacteria and archaea are prokaryotes. Eukaryotes have a nucleus and other organelles, and include animals, plants, fungi and protists. Eukaryotic cells are generally larger and more complex.

## Nucleus
The nucleus is the organelle that holds a eukaryotic cell's DNA and controls its activities. It is surrounded by a double membrane with pores that let molecules in and out. Red blood cells in mammals lose their nucleus as they mature.

## Mitochondria
Mitochondria are organelles that release energy from glucose through cellular respiration. They are sometimes called the powerhouses of the cell. Muscle and nerve cells, which need a lot of energy, contain many of them.

## Ribosome
A ribosome is the structure where proteins are built by joining amino acids in the order set by messenger RNA. Ribosomes are found in all cells, including bacteria. They are the target of many antibiotics.

## Chloroplast
A chloroplast is the organelle in plant and algae cells where photosynthesis takes place. It contains the green pigment chlorophyll, which absorbs light energy. Plants use the glucose made there as food.

## Cell membrane
The cell membrane is the thin layer that surrounds a cell and controls what enters and leaves. It is made of two layers of fat molecules with proteins in it. It is partly permeable, letting small molecules through.

## Cell wall
A cell wall is a strong outer layer around some cells that gives shape and protection. Plant cell walls are made mainly of cellulose; fungi have chitin, and bacteria have a different material. Animal cells do not have cell walls.

## Cytoplasm
The cytoplasm is the jelly-like fluid that fills a cell and surrounds its organelles. Many chemical reactions of the cell happen in it. It is mostly water with dissolved salts, sugars and proteins.

## DNA
DNA (deoxyribonucleic acid) is the molecule that carries the genetic instructions for building and running a living thing. It is shaped like a double helix, with two strands linked by pairs of bases: A with T and C with G. Humans have 46 DNA molecules in each normal body cell, one set from each parent.

## RNA
RNA (ribonucleic acid) is a single-stranded molecule that helps carry out the instructions in DNA. There are several kinds: messenger RNA carries a copy of a gene to the ribosome, and transfer RNA brings amino acids. RNA uses uracil in place of the DNA base thymine.

## Gene
A gene is a section of DNA that holds the instructions for making a protein or for controlling a cell process. Genes are passed from parents to offspring. Different versions of a gene are called alleles.

## Chromosome
A chromosome is a long molecule of DNA wrapped with proteins, which carries many genes. Humans have 46 chromosomes in each normal body cell, arranged in 23 pairs. Sex cells have 23 chromosomes each.

## Protein
A protein is a large molecule made of a chain of amino acids, folded into a shape that determines what it does. Proteins build structures, act as enzymes, carry oxygen and fight infections. The order of amino acids comes from the sequence of bases in DNA.

## Enzyme
An enzyme is a protein that speeds up a chemical reaction in a living thing without being used up. Each enzyme works best at a particular temperature and pH, and loses its shape when overheated. Enzymes digest food, copy DNA and build molecules.

## Mitosis
Mitosis is cell division in which one cell makes two genetically identical cells. It is used for growth and for replacing worn-out cells. In humans it keeps the chromosome number at 46.

## Meiosis
Meiosis is cell division that produces sex cells (gametes), each with half the normal chromosome number. Humans make egg and sperm cells with 23 chromosomes. Meiosis creates genetic variation, so no two gametes are exactly alike.

## Photosynthesis
Photosynthesis is how plants, algae and some bacteria turn light energy into chemical energy in glucose. Carbon dioxide and water are used, and oxygen is released. It takes place mainly in the chloroplasts of leaves.

## Cellular respiration
Cellular respiration is how cells release energy from glucose. It uses oxygen and produces carbon dioxide and water, and it happens mainly in the mitochondria. The energy is stored in a molecule called ATP, which cells use to do work.

## Evolution
Evolution is the change in the inherited characteristics of populations over generations. It leads to new species over long periods of time. Evidence comes from fossils, anatomy, genetics and the study of living species.

## Natural selection
Natural selection is the process by which individuals with characteristics better suited to their environment tend to survive and reproduce more. Their useful features become more common in later generations. It is the main mechanism of evolution.

## Species
A species is a group of living things that can breed together and produce fertile offspring. Members of a species share a common gene pool. Different species are usually kept apart by barriers to breeding.

## Kingdoms of life
Living things are commonly grouped into kingdoms: animals, plants, fungi, protists, and two groups of single-celled organisms, bacteria and archaea. Each kingdom groups organisms with a shared basic cell type and way of life. The exact grouping is still discussed by biologists.

## Bacteria
Bacteria are single-celled organisms with no nucleus, belonging to the prokaryotes. They are found almost everywhere, including in soil, water and the human gut. Some cause disease, but many are harmless or useful, such as those that make yoghurt.

## Virus
A virus is a tiny particle of genetic material inside a protein coat, and it can only reproduce inside a living cell. It is not usually considered a cell or fully alive. Viruses cause illnesses such as influenza and measles.

## Bacteria and viruses compared
Bacteria are living cells that can grow and reproduce on their own, while viruses are much smaller and need a host cell to multiply. Antibiotics kill bacteria but do not work on viruses. This is why viral illnesses are not treated with antibiotics.

## Circulatory system
The circulatory system moves blood around the body. It consists of the heart, blood vessels and blood, and carries oxygen, food molecules, hormones and waste. Its main circuit goes from the heart through the lungs and then around the body.

## Respiratory system
The respiratory system takes oxygen into the body and removes carbon dioxide. It includes the nose, windpipe, bronchi and lungs, where gas exchange happens in tiny air sacs. Oxygen then passes into the blood.

## Digestive system
The digestive system breaks food down into small molecules the body can absorb. It includes the mouth, oesophagus, stomach, small intestine and large intestine, plus organs such as the liver and pancreas. Absorbed nutrients pass into the blood.

## Nervous system
The nervous system senses the world and controls the body's responses. It is made of the brain, spinal cord and a network of nerves. Messages travel as electrical signals along neurons.

## Skeletal system
The skeletal system is the body's framework of bones, which supports the body, protects organs and allows movement with muscles. An adult human has 206 bones. Bone marrow inside bones makes blood cells.

## Muscular system
The muscular system is made of muscles that pull on bones to produce movement. There are three kinds: skeletal, smooth and cardiac muscle. Muscles can only pull, so they usually work in pairs.

## Immune system
The immune system defends the body against bacteria, viruses and other harmful things. It includes white blood cells, the skin and lymph organs. It can remember past infections, which is the basis of vaccination.

## Endocrine system
The endocrine system is a set of glands that release hormones into the blood. Hormones travel around the body and control growth, metabolism, reproduction and other processes. Examples are the thyroid and the adrenal glands.

## Heart
The heart is the muscular organ that pumps blood around the body. It has four chambers: two atria receive blood and two ventricles pump it out. It beats rhythmically without stopping, controlled by its own electrical signals.

## Lungs
The lungs are the pair of organs in the chest used for breathing. Air passes through branching tubes into tiny air sacs called alveoli, where oxygen enters the blood and carbon dioxide leaves it. The diaphragm muscle moves air in and out.

## Brain
The brain is the organ that controls the body and processes information from the senses. It is part of the central nervous system, protected inside the skull. Different regions handle movement, memory, language, emotion and many other functions.

## Blood components
Blood is made of plasma, a yellow liquid, plus red blood cells, white blood cells and platelets. Red cells carry oxygen using haemoglobin, white cells fight infection, and platelets help blood to clot. Plasma carries dissolved nutrients, hormones and waste.

## Blood groups
Blood is grouped by the ABO system into A, B, AB and O, and by the Rhesus (Rh) factor, which is either positive or negative. These groups depend on substances on the surface of red blood cells. Matching groups matters for blood transfusions.

## The five senses
The five traditional senses are sight, hearing, smell, taste and touch. Sight uses the eyes, hearing the ears, smell the nose, taste the tongue and touch the skin. Other senses, such as balance and temperature, are also recognised.

## Vitamin A
Vitamin A is needed for good vision, healthy skin and a working immune system. It is found in carrots, liver and dairy foods. A lack of it causes night blindness.

## Vitamin B12
Vitamin B12 is needed for healthy red blood cells and for the nervous system. It is found mainly in animal foods such as meat, eggs and dairy. A lack of it causes a type of anaemia.

## Vitamin C
Vitamin C is needed to make collagen, which supports skin, blood vessels and bones, and it helps the body absorb iron. It is found in fruit and vegetables such as citrus and peppers. A lack of it causes scurvy.

## Vitamin D
Vitamin D helps the body absorb calcium and is needed for strong bones and teeth. The skin makes it when exposed to sunlight, and it is also found in oily fish and egg yolks. A lack of it can lead to weak bones.

## Vitamin K
Vitamin K is needed for blood clotting. It is found in green leafy vegetables and is also made by bacteria in the gut. Without enough of it, blood takes longer to clot.

## Macronutrients
Macronutrients are the nutrients needed in large amounts: carbohydrates, proteins and fats, plus water. Carbohydrates give quick energy, proteins build and repair the body, and fats store energy and help the body absorb vitamins. The body needs a balance of all three.

## Micronutrients
Micronutrients are vitamins and minerals needed in small amounts for good health. They do not provide energy, but many are essential for body processes. Examples are iron, calcium and the vitamins.

## Ecosystem
An ecosystem is a community of living things together with the non-living parts of their environment, such as water, soil and sunlight. The living and non-living parts interact and depend on each other. Rainforests, ponds and grasslands are examples.

## Food chain
A food chain shows the order in which living things feed on one another, starting with a producer such as a plant. A rabbit eats the plant, a fox eats the rabbit. Energy passes along the chain and decreases at each step.

## Food web
A food web is a network of several connected food chains in an ecosystem. It shows how many different organisms eat each other. Removing one species can affect many others in the web.

## Biodiversity
Biodiversity is the variety of living things in a place, including the number of species and the genetic differences within them. Healthy ecosystems are usually rich in biodiversity. It can be lost through habitat destruction and other human activities.

## Vertebrate classes
Vertebrates are animals with a backbone, and they are grouped into five classes: fish, amphibians, reptiles, birds and mammals. Fish live in water and have gills; amphibians start life in water; reptiles have scaly skin; birds have feathers; mammals feed young on milk.

## Plant parts and their roles
The main plant parts are roots, stems, leaves, flowers and fruits. Roots absorb water and minerals and anchor the plant, stems carry water and support leaves, and leaves carry out photosynthesis. Flowers make seeds, which fruits enclose and protect.

## Diffusion and osmosis
Diffusion is the movement of particles from where there are many to where there are few, without a barrier. Osmosis is the movement of water across a partially permeable membrane towards the side with more dissolved particles. Both happen without the cell using energy.

## Homeostasis
Homeostasis is the way living things keep their internal conditions steady, such as body temperature and blood sugar. It works through regulation loops that detect change and respond to it. Healthy bodies hold many values within narrow limits.

## Mutation
A mutation is a change in the sequence of DNA. It can happen when DNA is copied or as a result of damage, such as from radiation. Some mutations have no effect, some cause disease or new traits, and some are useful for evolution.

## Genotype and phenotype
A genotype is the set of genetic versions an organism carries for a trait. A phenotype is the trait you can observe, such as eye colour. The phenotype depends on the genotype and often on the environment as well.

## Hormone
A hormone is a chemical messenger made by a gland and carried in the blood to control a body process. Hormones act on specific cells that have receptors for them. Insulin is a hormone that lowers blood sugar.

## Neuron
A neuron is a nerve cell that carries electrical signals through the nervous system. It has dendrites that receive signals, a cell body, and a long axon that passes signals on. Neurons connect into large networks.

## Antibiotic
An antibiotic is a medicine that kills bacteria or stops them growing. It does not work against viruses such as those that cause the common cold. Overusing antibiotics helps resistant bacteria survive.

## Fungi
Fungi are a kingdom of living things that feed by absorbing nutrients from their surroundings. They include mushrooms, moulds and yeasts, and their cells have chitin cell walls. Some fungi break down dead matter, while others, such as yeast, are useful in baking and brewing.

## Pollination
Pollination is the transfer of pollen from the male part of a flower to the female part. It allows fertilisation and the making of seeds. Wind, insects, birds and bats can all carry pollen.

## Adaptation
An adaptation is a feature that helps a living thing survive in its environment, such as thick fur in cold climates or webbed feet for swimming. Adaptations develop through natural selection over many generations. They are suited to a particular way of life, not perfect in general.
