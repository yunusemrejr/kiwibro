# Units and conversions

## Inch to centimetres (2.54 cm exactly)
One inch is exactly 2.54 centimetres. The inch is used for screen sizes, pipe diameters and, in the United States, for many everyday lengths; 12 inches make one foot.

## Foot to metres (0.3048 m exactly)
One foot is exactly 0.3048 metres, or 30.48 centimetres, and it contains 12 inches. The foot is still used for height and room sizes in the United States and Britain.

## Yard to metres (0.9144 m exactly)
One yard is exactly 0.9144 metres, which is three feet or 36 inches. It is used for cloth and for distances on some sports fields, such as a football pitch.

## Mile to kilometres (1.609344 km exactly)
One mile is exactly 1.609344 kilometres. A mile is 1,760 yards or 5,280 feet, and road distances in the United States and Britain are still given in miles.

## Kilometre to miles (0.621371 miles)
One kilometre is about 0.621371 miles, so 5 km is about 3.1 miles and 100 km is about 62 miles. A quick estimate is to multiply kilometres by 0.6, or to remember that 8 km is about 5 miles.

## Nautical mile to kilometres (1.852 km exactly)
One nautical mile is exactly 1.852 kilometres, and it equals one minute of latitude. It is the standard unit for distances at sea and in the air, and speeds in knots are nautical miles per hour.

## Metre to feet (3.28084 feet)
One metre is about 3.28 feet, or 39.37 inches. To convert the other way, multiply feet by 0.3048 to get metres.

## Millimetre to inches (0.03937 inches)
One millimetre is about 0.03937 inches, so 25.4 millimetres make one inch. A measurement in inches is roughly the number of millimetres divided by 25.

## Centimetre to inches (0.393701 inches)
One centimetre is about 0.3937 inches, so 2.54 centimetres make one inch. A person 180 cm tall is about 70.9 inches, or just under 5 feet 11 inches.

## Gram to ounces (0.035274 ounces)
One gram is about 0.0353 ounces, so 28.35 grams make one ounce. A 100-gram portion is about 3.5 ounces.

## Kilogram to stone (0.157473 stone)
One kilogram is about 0.157 stone, so 6.35 kilograms make one stone. A person of 80 kilograms weighs about 12 stone 8 pounds.

## Square metre to square feet (10.7639 square feet)
One square metre is about 10.764 square feet. A small flat of 50 square metres is about 538 square feet.

## Square inch to square centimetres (6.4516 cm²)
One square inch is exactly 6.4516 square centimetres. It is the unit for small areas such as the cross-section of a cable or the size of a screen's pixel grid.

## Cubic metre of water (1,000 kg)
One cubic metre of water has a mass of 1,000 kilograms, or one tonne, at its normal density. This link between volume and mass is why a litre of water weighs about one kilogram.

## Cubic foot to litres (28.3168 L)
One cubic foot is exactly 28.316846592 litres. It is used for the volume of rooms, fridges and deliveries in the United States, so a fridge of 20 cubic feet holds about 566 litres.

## Fluid ounce to tablespoons (2 tablespoons)
One US fluid ounce is 2 US tablespoons, or 6 US teaspoons. A small medicine cup marked in fluid ounces therefore also measures tablespoons.

## Pint of water (about 473 g, just over a pound)
A US pint of water weighs about 473 grams, a little over one pound, which is the origin of the saying "a pint's a pound the world around". An imperial pint of water weighs about 568 grams, or 1.25 pounds.

## Cup of flour (about 125 g)
One US cup of plain flour weighs about 125 grams, though it varies with how the flour is scooped. Bread flour and wholemeal flour weigh slightly more, and sifting makes a cup lighter.

## Bar to pounds per square inch (14.5038 psi)
One bar is about 14.504 pounds per square inch. So a tyre pressure of 2 bar is about 29 psi, and the usual atmospheric pressure of about one bar is about 14.7 psi.

## Millibar and hectopascal (the same size)
One millibar is exactly one hectopascal, or 100 pascals, and both are used in weather reports. Standard sea-level pressure is about 1,013 millibars, or 1,013 hectopascals.

## Point and pica (type sizes)
In printing and on computers, one inch is 72 points, and one pica is 12 points. Body text is often 10 to 12 points, and a font size given in a word processor is in points.

## Degree of latitude (about 111 km)
One degree of latitude is about 111 kilometres on the ground, and one minute of latitude is about 1.85 kilometres. The distance is nearly constant, which is why the nautical mile is defined from it.

## Fathom (1.83 metres, depth at sea)
One fathom is exactly 1.8288 metres, or 6 feet, and it is used for the depth of water. Charts in some countries still mark depth in fathoms.

## Centimetre and millimetre
One centimetre is 10 millimetres, and one metre is 100 centimetres or 1,000 millimetres. A fingernail is about a centimetre wide, and a millimetre is about the thickness of a bank card.

## Kilometre and metre
One kilometre is 1,000 metres. Walking at an ordinary pace covers about one kilometre in 12 minutes, and a kilometre is a little over half a mile.

## Pound to kilograms (0.45359237 kg exactly)
One pound is exactly 0.45359237 kilograms, or about 454 grams. A rough rule is that 2.2 pounds make one kilogram, so 10 stone, or 140 pounds, is about 63.5 kilograms.

## Kilogram to pounds (2.20462 pounds)
One kilogram is about 2.2046 pounds. To estimate, double the kilograms and add a tenth, so 70 kg is about 154 pounds.

## Ounce to grams (28.349523125 g exactly)
One ounce is exactly 28.349523125 grams, about 28.35 g. There are 16 ounces in a pound, and the ounce is used for food portions and postal weights in some countries.

## Stone to kilograms (6.35029318 kg)
One stone is 14 pounds, which is exactly 6.35029318 kilograms. Body weight in Britain and Ireland is often given in stones and pounds, so 11 stone is about 70 kilograms.

## Gram, milligram and kilogram
One gram is 1,000 milligrams, and one kilogram is 1,000 grams. A paperclip weighs about a gram, a teaspoon of sugar about 4 grams and a bag of flour about a kilogram.

## Tonne and ton (metric, short and long)
A metric tonne is 1,000 kilograms. The US short ton is 2,000 pounds, about 907 kg, and the British long ton is 2,240 pounds, about 1,016 kg, so the three are not the same.

## US gallon to litres (3.785411784 L exactly)
One US gallon is exactly 3.785411784 litres. It is the gallon used in the United States, and it is divided into four quarts, eight pints or 128 fluid ounces.

## Imperial gallon to litres (4.54609 L exactly)
One imperial gallon is exactly 4.54609 litres, about a fifth larger than the US gallon. It is the older British gallon, and fuel in Britain is now sold in litres but often quoted in miles per imperial gallon.

## US fluid ounce to millilitres (29.5735 mL)
One US fluid ounce is exactly 29.5735295625 millilitres, about 29.57 mL. It is a measure of volume, not weight, so a fluid ounce of water weighs about 29.6 grams.

## US pint to millilitres (473.176 mL)
One US pint is 16 US fluid ounces, exactly 473.176473 millilitres, about 473 mL. The British imperial pint is larger, at 568 millilitres, and is still used for beer in pubs.

## US quart to litres (0.946353 L)
One US quart is two US pints, exactly 0.946352946 litres, about 946 mL. Four quarts make one US gallon.

## US cup to millilitres (236.588 mL)
One US cup in cooking is 8 US fluid ounces, exactly 236.5882365 millilitres, about 237 mL. Metric recipes often treat a cup as 250 mL, which is close but not identical.

## Tablespoon to millilitres (14.787 mL in the US)
One US tablespoon is half a US fluid ounce, about 14.79 millilitres, and three teaspoons make one tablespoon. An Australian tablespoon is larger, at 20 millilitres.

## Teaspoon to millilitres (4.929 mL in the US)
One US teaspoon is about 4.93 millilitres, and 16 teaspoons make one US fluid ounce. A level teaspoon of sugar weighs about 4 grams.

## Imperial fluid ounce and pint
One imperial fluid ounce is 28.4130625 millilitres, and one imperial pint is 20 imperial fluid ounces, exactly 568.26125 millilitres. These are the older British measures, still used for draught beer.

## Litre, millilitre and cubic metre
One litre is 1,000 millilitres, and one cubic metre is 1,000 litres. A bottle of water is often half a litre, or 500 mL, and a bathtub holds roughly 150 litres.

## Acre to square metres (4,046.86 m²)
One acre is exactly 4,046.8564224 square metres, or about 0.405 hectares. It is often described as the area a team of oxen could plough in a day, roughly the size of a football pitch.

## Hectare to acres (2.471 acres)
One hectare is 10,000 square metres, a square 100 metres on each side. It is about 2.471 acres, and a field of 100 hectares is about one square kilometre.

## Square mile to square kilometres (2.58999 km²)
One square mile is exactly 2.589988110336 square kilometres. A square mile is 640 acres, and it is the unit used for large areas in the United States and Britain.

## Square foot and square yard
One square foot is exactly 0.09290304 square metres, and one square yard is exactly 0.83612736 square metres, or 9 square feet. Floor areas in some countries are given in square feet.

## Knot to kilometres per hour (1.852 km/h)
One knot is one nautical mile per hour, exactly 1.852 kilometres per hour. Wind speed and boat speed are given in knots, so 20 knots is about 37 km/h.

## Miles per hour to kilometres per hour (× 1.609344)
To change miles per hour into kilometres per hour, multiply by 1.609344. A speed limit of 30 mph is about 48 km/h, 60 mph is about 97 km/h and 70 mph is about 113 km/h.

## Kilometres per hour to miles per hour (× 0.621371)
To change kilometres per hour into miles per hour, multiply by 0.621371. A limit of 50 km/h is about 31 mph, 100 km/h is about 62 mph and 120 km/h is about 75 mph.

## Metres per second to kilometres per hour (× 3.6)
One metre per second is exactly 3.6 kilometres per hour. Wind speeds in weather reports are often given in metres per second, so 10 m/s is 36 km/h.

## Celsius to Fahrenheit (formula)
To change Celsius into Fahrenheit, multiply by 9, divide by 5 and add 32. So 0 °C is 32 °F, 20 °C is 68 °F, 37 °C is about 98.6 °F and 100 °C is 212 °F.

## Fahrenheit to Celsius (formula)
To change Fahrenheit into Celsius, subtract 32, multiply by 5 and divide by 9. So 32 °F is 0 °C, 98.6 °F is 37 °C and 212 °F is 100 °C.

## Celsius to Kelvin (formula)
To change Celsius into kelvin, add 273.15. So 0 °C is 273.15 K, and absolute zero is 0 K, which is -273.15 °C. Kelvin is used in science because it has no negative numbers.

## Calorie to joules (4.184 J)
One calorie is exactly 4.184 joules, and a food Calorie, written with a capital, is a kilocalorie, or 4,184 joules. Energy on food labels is often given in both.

## Kilowatt-hour to megajoules (3.6 MJ)
One kilowatt-hour is exactly 3.6 megajoules, the energy used by a 1,000-watt appliance running for one hour. Electricity bills are usually charged in kilowatt-hours.

## Horsepower to watts (about 745.7 W)
One mechanical horsepower is about 745.7 watts, the unit used for car engines in some countries. A metric horsepower is slightly smaller, at about 735.5 watts.

## Bar, atmosphere and pascal
One bar is exactly 100,000 pascals, and one standard atmosphere is 101,325 pascals, or 1.01325 bar. Normal air pressure at sea level is about one bar, and tyre pressure is often given in bar or psi.

## Pounds per square inch to bar (0.06895 bar)
One pound per square inch (psi) is about 0.06895 bar, or 6,895 pascals. A car tyre is often filled to about 32 psi, which is about 2.2 bar.

## Byte and bit (8 bits)
One byte is 8 bits. A bit is a single 0 or 1, and a byte is the unit used for file sizes and memory, so a byte can store one character of plain text.

## Kilobyte and kibibyte (1,000 vs 1,024 bytes)
In the decimal system one kilobyte is 1,000 bytes, written KB. In the binary system one kibibyte is 1,024 bytes, written KiB, and operating systems often show the binary number while labels show the decimal one.

## Megabyte and mebibyte (1,000,000 vs 1,048,576 bytes)
One megabyte is 1,000,000 bytes in the decimal system, written MB. One mebibyte is 1,048,576 bytes, or 1,024 kibibytes, written MiB; the difference is about 5 per cent.

## Gigabyte and gibibyte (1,000,000,000 vs 1,073,741,824 bytes)
One gigabyte is 1,000,000,000 bytes, written GB, and one gibibyte is 1,073,741,824 bytes, written GiB. A phone advertised with 128 GB may show about 119 GiB of space in some systems.

## Terabyte and tebibyte (1,000,000,000,000 vs 1,099,511,627,776 bytes)
One terabyte is 1,000,000,000,000 bytes, written TB, and one tebibyte is 1,099,511,627,776 bytes, written TiB. Hard drive makers use the decimal units and many operating systems use the binary ones.

## Megabits per second and megabytes per second (divide by 8)
Internet speed is often given in megabits per second (Mbps), while file sizes are in megabytes (MB). Divide by 8 to compare, so a 100 Mbps connection downloads at about 12.5 MB per second.

## Time units (second, minute, hour, day)
One minute is 60 seconds, one hour is 60 minutes or 3,600 seconds, and one day is 24 hours or 86,400 seconds. A week is 7 days and a fortnight is 14 days.

## Month and year lengths
A common year has 365 days and a leap year 366. Months are 28 to 31 days long, and the average month over a year is about 30.44 days; the average year in the Gregorian calendar is 365.2425 days.

## Decade, century and millennium
A decade is 10 years, a century is 100 years and a millennium is 1,000 years. The 20th century ran from 1901 to 2000, and the 21st century began on 1 January 2001.

## Light-year (distance light travels in a year)
One light-year is about 9.46 trillion kilometres, the distance light travels in one year. It is a measure of distance, not time, and the nearest star after the sun is about 4.2 light-years away.

## Astronomical unit (Earth to the Sun)
One astronomical unit is about 149.6 million kilometres, the average distance from the Earth to the Sun. Distances inside the solar system are often given in these units, so Neptune is about 30 astronomical units away.

## A-series paper sizes (A0 to A6)
A-series paper is based on A0, which has an area of one square metre and sides of 841 by 1,189 mm. Each smaller size is half the previous one: A1 is 594 by 841 mm, A2 420 by 594 mm, A3 297 by 420 mm, A4 210 by 297 mm, A5 148 by 210 mm and A6 105 by 148 mm.

## A4 paper (210 by 297 mm)
A4 is the most common paper size in most of the world, 210 by 297 millimetres, close to 8.27 by 11.69 inches. It is used for letters, printing and photocopies outside the United States and Canada.

## US Letter paper (8.5 by 11 inches)
US Letter paper is 8.5 by 11 inches, or 216 by 279 millimetres, slightly wider and shorter than A4. It is the standard office paper in the United States and Canada.

## US Legal paper (8.5 by 14 inches)
US Legal paper is 8.5 by 14 inches, or 216 by 356 millimetres, longer than US Letter. It is used for legal documents and contracts in the United States.

## Dozen and baker's dozen
A dozen is 12 items, and a baker's dozen is 13. Eggs and bread rolls are commonly sold by the dozen.

## Gross and great gross
A gross is 144 items, or 12 dozen, and a great gross is 1,728 items, or 12 gross. The words are still used for small manufactured parts sold in bulk.

## Score (a count of 20)
A score is 20 items. The phrase "four score and seven years ago" in the American Gettysburg Address means 87 years.

## Carat (weight of gemstones)
One carat is 200 milligrams, or 0.2 grams, and it is the unit for the weight of diamonds and other gemstones. The word is different from karat, which measures the purity of gold.

## Karat (purity of gold)
Karat measures the purity of gold in 24 parts, so 24-karat gold is pure and 18-karat gold is 18 parts gold to 6 parts other metal. In Britain and some other countries the same idea is written with a c, as carat.

## Metric prefixes (kilo, milli and the rest)
Metric prefixes multiply or divide a unit by powers of ten: kilo means a thousand, centi a hundredth, milli a thousandth, micro a millionth and nano a billionth. Larger ones are mega for a million, giga for a billion and tera for a trillion.

## Metric and imperial systems (the difference)
The metric system is based on decimal multiples such as metre, litre and gram, and is used in most countries. The imperial system, used mainly in the United States, is based on inches, feet, pounds and gallons, which do not divide by ten.

## Oven temperatures (Celsius, Fahrenheit and gas mark)
British recipes often give a gas mark, American recipes give Fahrenheit and most of the world gives Celsius. Gas mark 1 is 140 °C or 275 °F, mark 4 is 180 °C or 350 °F and mark 6 is 200 °C or 400 °F; each mark is roughly 10 to 20 °C apart.

## Gas mark conversions (marks 1 to 9)
The common gas marks are: 1 is 140 °C, 2 is 150 °C, 3 is 170 °C, 4 is 180 °C, 5 is 190 °C, 6 is 200 °C, 7 is 220 °C, 8 is 230 °C and 9 is 240 °C. In Fahrenheit these run from 275 °F at mark 1 to 475 °F at mark 9.

## Stick of butter (113 g)
A US stick of butter weighs 113 grams, which is 4 ounces, half a US cup or 8 tablespoons. In countries that sell butter in blocks, 113 g is a little under half of a 250 g block.

## Cup of water (about 237 g)
One US cup of water weighs about 237 grams, because a millilitre of water weighs about one gram. The same cup of flour weighs only about 125 grams and of sugar about 200 grams, so cups are not a reliable measure for dry goods.

## Tablespoon of butter or sugar (about 14 g)
One US tablespoon of butter weighs about 14 grams, and one tablespoon of granulated sugar also weighs about 12 to 14 grams. For accuracy in baking, weights in grams are more reliable than spoons.
