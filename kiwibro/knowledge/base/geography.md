# Geography of the world

## Asia (continent)
Asia is the largest of the seven continents, covering the eastern part of the Eurasian landmass. It runs from the Arctic in the north to the Indian Ocean in the south and from the Pacific in the east to Europe in the west, and it holds the highest mountains on Earth.

## Africa (continent)
Africa is the second largest continent. It lies south of Europe, between the Atlantic Ocean to the west and the Indian Ocean to the east, and the equator and both tropics cross it.

## North America (continent)
North America is a continent in the northern hemisphere, between the Pacific and Atlantic oceans. It includes Canada, the United States and Mexico, and the region of Central America and the Caribbean islands is usually counted with it.

## South America (continent)
South America is a continent in the western hemisphere, joined to North America by the narrow isthmus of Panama. It is crossed by the equator in the north and by the Andes mountains along its western edge.

## Antarctica (continent)
Antarctica is the continent around the South Pole, almost entirely covered by ice. It is the coldest place on Earth and has no permanent residents, only research stations run by many countries.

## Europe (continent)
Europe is a continent in the northern hemisphere, usually taken as the western part of Eurasia. Its eastern limit is commonly placed at the Ural mountains in Russia, and it is bordered by the Arctic Ocean, the Atlantic and the Mediterranean Sea.

## Oceania (continent and region)
Oceania is the region made up of Australia, New Zealand, New Guinea and the island groups of the Pacific. When counted as a continent, it is the smallest of the seven.

## Largest continent (Asia)
Asia is the largest continent by both area and number of people. It stretches across about a third of the world's land and contains China, India and most of Russia.

## Smallest continent (Australia)
Australia is the smallest continent, and is often grouped with the Pacific islands as Oceania. It is also the only continent that is a single country.

## Pacific Ocean
The Pacific is the largest and deepest of the oceans. It lies between Asia and Australia on the west and the Americas on the east, and it contains the deepest known point in the sea, the Challenger Deep in the Mariana Trench.

## Atlantic Ocean
The Atlantic is the second largest ocean. It separates the Americas from Europe and Africa and has an S shape, running from the Arctic to the Southern Ocean.

## Indian Ocean
The Indian Ocean is the third largest ocean. Africa lies to its west, Asia to its north and Australia to its east, and it is bounded to the south by the Southern Ocean.

## Southern Ocean
The Southern Ocean is the water that surrounds Antarctica, generally taken as the sea south of about 60 degrees south latitude. It is counted as an ocean by many countries and organisations, and it connects the Atlantic, Pacific and Indian oceans.

## Arctic Ocean
The Arctic Ocean is the smallest and shallowest ocean, around the North Pole. Much of it is covered by sea ice, and it is bordered by northern Russia, Canada, Alaska, Greenland and Scandinavia.

## Largest ocean (Pacific)
The Pacific is the largest ocean, covering about a third of the Earth's surface. It is also the deepest ocean, with an average depth of several thousand metres.

## Smallest ocean (Arctic)
The Arctic Ocean is the smallest of the five oceans. It lies around the North Pole and is largely covered by floating sea ice.

## Mediterranean Sea
The Mediterranean Sea lies between Europe to the north, Africa to the south and Asia to the east. It connects to the Atlantic Ocean through the narrow Strait of Gibraltar and to the Red Sea through the Suez Canal.

## Black Sea
The Black Sea lies between south-eastern Europe and Western Asia. It is almost enclosed by land, joins the Mediterranean through the Bosphorus strait, and receives the water of the Danube and other large rivers.

## Red Sea
The Red Sea is a long, narrow sea between Africa and the Arabian peninsula. It joins the Indian Ocean through the Bab el-Mandeb strait and the Mediterranean through the Suez Canal, and it is one of the saltiest seas.

## Caspian Sea
The Caspian Sea is the largest inland body of water in the world, a salt lake with no outlet to any ocean. Russia, Kazakhstan, Turkmenistan, Iran and Azerbaijan border it.

## North Sea
The North Sea lies between Britain and the coasts of Norway, Denmark, Germany and the Netherlands. It is part of the Atlantic Ocean and is shallow compared with the open ocean.

## Baltic Sea
The Baltic Sea lies in northern Europe, bordered by Sweden, Finland, Russia, Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania, Poland, Germany and Denmark. It connects to the North Sea through the Danish straits and its water is only slightly salty.

## Caribbean Sea
The Caribbean Sea lies between Central America to the west, the Caribbean islands to the north and east and South America to the south. It is part of the Atlantic Ocean and contains many coral reefs and islands.

## South China Sea
The South China Sea is a part of the western Pacific Ocean. It lies between China and Taiwan to the north, the Philippines to the east, Borneo and Malaysia to the south and Vietnam to the west.

## Persian Gulf
The Persian Gulf lies between the Arabian peninsula and Iran. It holds large oil reserves, and it joins the Indian Ocean through the Strait of Hormuz and the Gulf of Oman.

## Bay of Bengal
The Bay of Bengal is a large bay of the north-eastern Indian Ocean. It lies between India and Sri Lanka to the west and Myanmar to the east, and the Ganges and Brahmaputra rivers flow into it.

## Gulf of Mexico
The Gulf of Mexico is a large gulf of the Atlantic Ocean. It is bordered by the United States to the north, Mexico to the west and south and Cuba to the east.

## Sea of Japan
The Sea of Japan lies between the island of Japan to the east and Russia and the Korean peninsula to the west. It is also called the East Sea, and it connects to the Pacific through narrow straits.

## Dead Sea (lowest land point)
The Dead Sea is a salt lake between Israel and Jordan, at the mouth of the Jordan River. Its shore is the lowest land surface on Earth, more than 400 metres below sea level, and it is so salty that a person floats easily in it.

## Nile (longest river, Africa)
The Nile flows north through north-eastern Africa and empties into the Mediterranean Sea. It is usually listed as the longest river in the world, together with the Amazon, and it is the main source of water for Egypt and Sudan.

## Amazon (river, South America)
The Amazon flows east across South America, mostly through Brazil, and empties into the Atlantic Ocean. It carries more water than any other river and drains the largest rainforest in the world.

## Yangtze (longest river in Asia)
The Yangtze is the longest river in Asia. It rises in the highlands of western China, flows east across the country and reaches the sea near Shanghai.

## Mississippi and Missouri (great river of North America)
The Mississippi and its longest tributary, the Missouri, form the great river system of North America. The combined system flows south through the United States and empties into the Gulf of Mexico.

## Volga (longest river in Europe)
The Volga is the longest river in Europe. It flows through western Russia and empties into the Caspian Sea, and it is an important route for shipping and water supply.

## Murray and Darling (river system of Australia)
The Murray and Darling rivers form the longest river system in Australia. They flow through the south-east of the country, and the Murray reaches the sea near Adelaide.

## Danube (river of central Europe)
The Danube flows from southern Germany eastwards through Austria, Hungary, Serbia and Romania and empties into the Black Sea. It passes through Vienna, Budapest and Belgrade and is used heavily for shipping.

## Congo (river of Central Africa)
The Congo flows through Central Africa and empties into the Atlantic Ocean. It carries more water than any other river in Africa and drains the Congo rainforest.

## Ganges (river of South Asia)
The Ganges flows east from the Himalayas through northern India and into Bangladesh, where it joins the Brahmaputra and empties into the Bay of Bengal. It is a sacred river for Hindus.

## Mount Everest (highest, tallest mountain in the world)
Mount Everest is the highest mountain above sea level, 8,849 metres tall, on the border of Nepal and China in the Himalayas. Its height was measured jointly by Nepal and China and announced in 2020.

## Aconcagua (highest mountain in South America)
Aconcagua is the highest mountain in the Americas and the highest outside Asia, at about 6,961 metres. It stands in the Andes in Argentina.

## Denali (highest mountain in North America)
Denali, formerly called Mount McKinley, is the highest mountain in North America at about 6,190 metres. It stands in Alaska, in the Alaska Range.

## Kilimanjaro (highest mountain in Africa)
Kilimanjaro is the highest mountain in Africa at about 5,895 metres. It is a dormant volcano in Tanzania, standing alone above the surrounding plains and topped by a shrinking glacier.

## Elbrus (highest mountain in Europe)
Mount Elbrus is the highest mountain in Europe at about 5,642 metres. It is a volcano in the Caucasus mountains in southern Russia, and its summit is covered in snow and ice.

## Vinson Massif (highest mountain in Antarctica)
Vinson Massif is the highest mountain in Antarctica at about 4,892 metres. It lies in the Ellsworth mountains and is climbed only by expeditions.

## Puncak Jaya (highest peak in Oceania)
Puncak Jaya is the highest peak in Oceania at about 4,884 metres. It lies in the highlands of New Guinea in Indonesia and carries a small glacier near its summit.

## Mount Kosciuszko (highest mountain in mainland Australia)
Mount Kosciuszko is the highest mountain in mainland Australia at 2,228 metres. It lies in the Snowy Mountains in New South Wales and is a gentle walk-up rather than a technical climb.

## Sahara (largest hot desert)
The Sahara is the largest hot desert in the world, covering much of North Africa. It is mostly rock and gravel with great sandy areas, and it stretches from the Atlantic to the Red Sea.

## Antarctic Desert (largest desert)
The Antarctic Desert is the largest desert on Earth. It is a polar desert: almost all of Antarctica is covered by ice and receives very little rain or snow as precipitation.

## Arabian Desert
The Arabian Desert covers most of the Arabian peninsula. It includes the Rub al Khali, a vast sand sea, and is one of the driest and hottest regions on Earth.

## Gobi Desert
The Gobi is a large desert in Mongolia and northern China. It is a cold desert, high above sea level, with harsh winters and very little rain.

## Kalahari Desert
The Kalahari is a large desert in southern Africa, covering parts of Botswana, Namibia and South Africa. Large areas of it are covered with grass and bushes rather than bare sand.

## Atacama Desert
The Atacama Desert runs along the coast of northern Chile. It is one of the driest places on Earth, with some areas receiving no measurable rain for years.

## Lake Superior (largest freshwater lake by area)
Lake Superior is the largest freshwater lake in the world by surface area. It lies between the United States and Canada and is one of the five Great Lakes.

## Lake Victoria (largest lake in Africa)
Lake Victoria is the largest lake in Africa by area. It lies between Uganda, Kenya and Tanzania and is the source of the White Nile.

## Lake Baikal (deepest lake)
Lake Baikal in Siberia is the deepest lake in the world, over 1,600 metres deep. It holds about a fifth of the world's unfrozen fresh water and is famous for its clear ice.

## Great Lakes (North America)
The Great Lakes are five large lakes between Canada and the United States. They are Superior, Michigan, Huron, Erie and Ontario, and together they hold the largest group of freshwater lakes on Earth.

## Himalayas (mountain range)
The Himalayas are a mountain range in Asia, running along the border of India, Nepal, Bhutan and China. They contain Mount Everest and the highest peaks in the world, and were pushed up by the collision of the Indian and Eurasian plates.

## Andes (mountain range)
The Andes are a long mountain range along the western edge of South America. They run through Colombia, Ecuador, Peru, Bolivia, Chile and Argentina and form the longest continental mountain range in the world.

## Rocky Mountains (mountain range)
The Rocky Mountains are a major range in North America. They run from northern British Columbia in Canada south through the United States to New Mexico, and they divide the rivers that flow to the Pacific from those that flow east.

## Alps (mountain range)
The Alps are a mountain range in central Europe. They run through France, Switzerland, Italy, Austria, Germany and Slovenia, and they are a centre of skiing and mountaineering.

## Urals (mountain range)
The Ural mountains run north to south through Russia and are usually taken as the boundary between Europe and Asia. They are old and rounded rather than high and sharp.

## Great Dividing Range (mountain range of Australia)
The Great Dividing Range runs along the eastern side of Australia, from Queensland through New South Wales into Victoria. It separates the coastal strip from the dry inland and is the source of many rivers.

## Atlas Mountains (mountain range of North Africa)
The Atlas mountains run across Morocco, Algeria and Tunisia in north-west Africa. They separate the Mediterranean coast from the Sahara and are the highest mountains in North Africa.

## Equator (line of latitude)
The equator is the imaginary line around the Earth at 0 degrees latitude, halfway between the North and South Poles. It divides the Earth into the northern and southern hemispheres and is about 40,075 kilometres long.

## Tropic of Cancer
The Tropic of Cancer is an imaginary line of latitude about 23.4 degrees north of the equator. It marks the northern limit of the places where the sun can be directly overhead, and it crosses Africa, Asia and North America.

## Tropic of Capricorn
The Tropic of Capricorn is an imaginary line of latitude about 23.4 degrees south of the equator. It marks the southern limit of the places where the sun can be directly overhead, and it crosses South America, Africa and Australia.

## North Pole
The North Pole is the point at the top of the Earth, at 90 degrees north latitude. It lies in the Arctic Ocean on shifting sea ice, and every direction from it is south.

## South Pole
The South Pole is the point at the bottom of the Earth, at 90 degrees south latitude. It lies on the ice sheet of Antarctica, where a research station stands, and every direction from it is north.

## Prime Meridian (0 degrees longitude)
The Prime Meridian is the line of 0 degrees longitude, running from the North Pole to the South Pole. By international agreement it passes through Greenwich in London, and longitudes east and west are measured from it.

## Latitude (how far north or south)
Latitude is the distance of a place north or south of the equator, measured in degrees from 0 at the equator to 90 at each pole. Lines of latitude run east to west around the Earth and are parallel to each other.

## Longitude (how far east or west)
Longitude is the distance of a place east or west of the Prime Meridian, measured in degrees from 0 to 180 in each direction. Lines of longitude run from pole to pole and are farthest apart at the equator.

## Time zones and UTC
The Earth turns through 360 degrees in 24 hours, so each 15 degrees of longitude is about one hour of time difference. Time zones are usually one hour apart, and their times are given as an offset from UTC, the world reference time.

## UTC (Coordinated Universal Time)
UTC is the world's reference time, the modern form of Greenwich Mean Time. It does not change with the seasons, so time zones are written as UTC plus or minus a number of hours, such as UTC+3 or UTC-5.

## International Date Line
The International Date Line is an imaginary line in the Pacific Ocean, roughly along 180 degrees longitude, where the date changes. Crossing it going west you add a day, and crossing it going east you subtract a day.

## Climate zones (tropical, dry, temperate, continental, polar)
Geographers divide climates into five main groups: tropical, dry, temperate, continental and polar. Each group is defined by temperature and rainfall patterns, and mountains create their own cooler zones at height.

## Tectonic plates
The outer shell of the Earth is broken into large slabs called tectonic plates, which move slowly over the mantle at a few centimetres a year. Earthquakes, volcanoes and mountain building happen mostly where plates meet.

## Volcano (how it works)
A volcano is an opening in the Earth's crust where molten rock, ash and gas reach the surface from below. Many volcanoes stand around the Pacific Ocean in a belt called the Ring of Fire, where plates are pushing under one another.

## Earthquake (what happens)
An earthquake is a sudden shaking of the ground caused by rocks slipping past each other along a fault in the Earth's crust. The energy travels out as seismic waves, and the shaking is strongest near the point where the slip happened.

## Earthquake magnitude scale (how strength is measured)
Earthquake strength is given by a magnitude number on a logarithmic scale, today usually the moment magnitude scale. Each whole number step means about 32 times more energy released, so a magnitude 6 quake is far stronger than a magnitude 5, and most quakes are too small to feel.

## Water cycle (evaporation, rain, runoff)
The water cycle is the constant movement of water between the oceans, the air and the land. Water evaporates, rises and condenses into clouds, falls as rain or snow, and then runs back to the sea through rivers or soaks into the ground.

## Layers of the Earth (core, mantle, crust)
The Earth has a solid inner core of iron and nickel, a liquid outer core, a thick rocky mantle and a thin crust on the outside. The outer core's movement produces the Earth's magnetic field, and the plates of the crust float on the mantle.

## Layers of the atmosphere (troposphere to exosphere)
The atmosphere has layers: the troposphere, where weather happens; the stratosphere, which holds the ozone layer; the mesosphere; the thermosphere; and the outer exosphere. Air pressure and temperature change with height, and the air thins until it fades into space.

## Weather and climate (the difference)
Weather is the state of the air at a particular place and time, such as today's rain or sunshine. Climate is the average pattern of weather over a long period, usually about 30 years, so a cold day does not contradict a warming climate.

## Compass directions (north, east, south, west)
A compass shows four main directions: north, east, south and west, in that order clockwise. Halfway between them are north-east, south-east, south-west and north-west, and a bearing gives a direction as degrees clockwise from north, from 0 to 360.
