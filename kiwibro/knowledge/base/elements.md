# Chemical elements reference

## Hydrogen (H), element 1
Hydrogen has the chemical symbol H and atomic number 1. It is a reactive nonmetal and the lightest and most abundant element in the universe. Stars are mostly hydrogen, and hydrogen combines with oxygen to form water.

## Helium (He), element 2
Helium has the chemical symbol He and atomic number 2. It is a noble gas. It is the second most abundant element in the universe, and being very light and unreactive it is used in balloons and to cool scientific equipment.

## Lithium (Li), element 3
Lithium has the chemical symbol Li and atomic number 3. It is an alkali metal and the lightest metal. It is soft enough to cut with a knife, and it is the key material in lithium-ion rechargeable batteries.

## Beryllium (Be), element 4
Beryllium has the chemical symbol Be and atomic number 4. It is an alkaline earth metal. It is light and stiff, so it is used in aerospace parts, and it is almost transparent to X-rays, which suits it to X-ray windows.

## Boron (B), element 5
Boron has the chemical symbol B and atomic number 5. It is a metalloid. It is used in borosilicate glass that resists heat, and in detergents and fibreglass.

## Carbon (C), element 6
Carbon has the chemical symbol C and atomic number 6. It is a reactive nonmetal and the basis of all known life. Diamond, one of the hardest substances, and graphite, the soft material in pencils, are both pure carbon.

## Nitrogen (N), element 7
Nitrogen has the chemical symbol N and atomic number 7. It is a reactive nonmetal. It makes up about 78% of the air we breathe, and its compounds are essential to fertilisers and to proteins in living things.

## Oxygen (O), element 8
Oxygen has the chemical symbol O and atomic number 8. It is a reactive nonmetal. It makes up about 21% of the air and is needed for combustion and for respiration in most living things.

## Fluorine (F), element 9
Fluorine has the chemical symbol F and atomic number 9. It is a halogen and the most reactive of all elements. Small amounts of fluoride are added to toothpaste and drinking water to help protect teeth.

## Neon (Ne), element 10
Neon has the chemical symbol Ne and atomic number 10. It is a noble gas. It glows a bright orange-red when electricity passes through it, which is the basis of traditional neon signs.

## Sodium (Na), element 11
Sodium has the chemical symbol Na and atomic number 11. It is an alkali metal. It reacts violently with water, and it is best known as half of table salt, sodium chloride.

## Magnesium (Mg), element 12
Magnesium has the chemical symbol Mg and atomic number 12. It is an alkaline earth metal. It burns with a brilliant white flame, which is why it was used in old photographic flash powder and is still used in fireworks.

## Aluminium (Al), element 13
Aluminium has the chemical symbol Al and atomic number 13. It is a post-transition metal. It is the most abundant metal in the Earth's crust, light and resistant to corrosion, and is used in drink cans, aircraft and foil.

## Silicon (Si), element 14
Silicon has the chemical symbol Si and atomic number 14. It is a metalloid. It is the main element in sand and rock, and purified silicon is the material of computer chips and solar cells.

## Phosphorus (P), element 15
Phosphorus has the chemical symbol P and atomic number 15. It is a reactive nonmetal. It is essential in DNA and bones, and white phosphorus glows faintly in air, which is the origin of its name, meaning light-bringer.

## Sulfur (S), element 16
Sulfur has the chemical symbol S and atomic number 16. It is a reactive nonmetal. It is a yellow solid used to make sulfuric acid, and its compound hydrogen sulfide gives rotten eggs their smell.

## Chlorine (Cl), element 17
Chlorine has the chemical symbol Cl and atomic number 17. It is a halogen. It is a greenish-yellow gas used to disinfect drinking water and swimming pools, and it forms table salt with sodium.

## Argon (Ar), element 18
Argon has the chemical symbol Ar and atomic number 18. It is a noble gas. It makes up about 1% of the air and is used to fill light bulbs and to protect metal during welding.

## Potassium (K), element 19
Potassium has the chemical symbol K and atomic number 19. It is an alkali metal. It is essential for nerve signals and heart function, and bananas are a well-known dietary source.

## Calcium (Ca), element 20
Calcium has the chemical symbol Ca and atomic number 20. It is an alkaline earth metal. It builds bones and teeth, and its compounds form limestone, chalk and marble.

## Scandium (Sc), element 21
Scandium has the chemical symbol Sc and atomic number 21. It is a transition metal. It is added to aluminium to make strong, light alloys for bicycle frames and aerospace parts.

## Titanium (Ti), element 22
Titanium has the chemical symbol Ti and atomic number 22. It is a transition metal. It is strong, light and resistant to corrosion, so it is used in aircraft, artificial joints and dental implants.

## Vanadium (V), element 23
Vanadium has the chemical symbol V and atomic number 23. It is a transition metal. Small amounts added to steel make it stronger and tougher, and its compounds are used as catalysts.

## Chromium (Cr), element 24
Chromium has the chemical symbol Cr and atomic number 24. It is a transition metal. It gives stainless steel its resistance to rust, and its shiny plating is used on taps and car parts.

## Manganese (Mn), element 25
Manganese has the chemical symbol Mn and atomic number 25. It is a transition metal. It is an essential ingredient in steel making, helping to remove oxygen and improve strength.

## Iron (Fe), element 26
Iron has the chemical symbol Fe and atomic number 26. It is a transition metal. It is the main ingredient of steel, the most widely used metal, and iron in haemoglobin carries oxygen in human blood.

## Cobalt (Co), element 27
Cobalt has the chemical symbol Co and atomic number 27. It is a transition metal. Its compounds have long been used for deep blue pigments, and it is a key component of many lithium-ion battery electrodes.

## Nickel (Ni), element 28
Nickel has the chemical symbol Ni and atomic number 28. It is a transition metal. It is used in stainless steel, coins and rechargeable batteries, and it resists corrosion well.

## Copper (Cu), element 29
Copper has the chemical symbol Cu and atomic number 29. It is a transition metal. It conducts electricity very well and is the standard metal for electrical wiring, and with tin it forms bronze.

## Zinc (Zn), element 30
Zinc has the chemical symbol Zn and atomic number 30. It is a transition metal. It is used to coat steel to stop it rusting, a process called galvanising, and with copper it forms brass.

## Gallium (Ga), element 31
Gallium has the chemical symbol Ga and atomic number 31. It is a post-transition metal. It melts at about 30 degrees Celsius, so a piece of it melts in a warm hand, and it is used in LEDs and semiconductors.

## Germanium (Ge), element 32
Germanium has the chemical symbol Ge and atomic number 32. It is a metalloid. It was used in the first transistors and is now used in infrared optics and some fibre-optic systems.

## Arsenic (As), element 33
Arsenic has the chemical symbol As and atomic number 33. It is a metalloid. It is poisonous and was once a notorious poison, and it is used in some semiconductors and wood preservatives.

## Selenium (Se), element 34
Selenium has the chemical symbol Se and atomic number 34. It is a reactive nonmetal. It is used in photocopiers and in glassmaking, and it is a trace nutrient needed in very small amounts.

## Bromine (Br), element 35
Bromine has the chemical symbol Br and atomic number 35. It is a halogen. It is a red-brown liquid at room temperature, one of only two elements that are liquid in normal conditions.

## Krypton (Kr), element 36
Krypton has the chemical symbol Kr and atomic number 36. It is a noble gas. It is used in high-performance lamps and photographic flash tubes, and a krypton light once defined the length of the metre.

## Rubidium (Rb), element 37
Rubidium has the chemical symbol Rb and atomic number 37. It is an alkali metal. It is used in atomic clocks and in some lasers, and it is soft and reacts readily with water.

## Strontium (Sr), element 38
Strontium has the chemical symbol Sr and atomic number 38. It is an alkaline earth metal. Its compounds give fireworks and flares a brilliant red colour.

## Yttrium (Y), element 39
Yttrium has the chemical symbol Y and atomic number 39. It is a transition metal. It is used in LEDs, superconductors and strong magnets, often in small amounts within other materials.

## Zirconium (Zr), element 40
Zirconium has the chemical symbol Zr and atomic number 40. It is a transition metal. It resists corrosion and absorbs few neutrons, so it is used to clad nuclear fuel rods.

## Niobium (Nb), element 41
Niobium has the chemical symbol Nb and atomic number 41. It is a transition metal. It is used in superconducting magnets and in special steels for pipelines and jet engines.

## Molybdenum (Mo), element 42
Molybdenum has the chemical symbol Mo and atomic number 42. It is a transition metal. It keeps its strength at high temperatures, so it is used in high-temperature alloys and in lubricants.

## Technetium (Tc), element 43
Technetium has the chemical symbol Tc and atomic number 43. It is a transition metal and the first element to be produced artificially. It has no stable isotopes, but one of them is widely used in medical imaging.

## Ruthenium (Ru), element 44
Ruthenium has the chemical symbol Ru and atomic number 44. It is a transition metal. It is a hard member of the platinum group, used in electrical contacts and as a catalyst.

## Rhodium (Rh), element 45
Rhodium has the chemical symbol Rh and atomic number 45. It is a transition metal. It is a rare, shiny platinum-group metal used in catalytic converters to clean car exhaust.

## Palladium (Pd), element 46
Palladium has the chemical symbol Pd and atomic number 46. It is a transition metal. It is used in catalytic converters and jewellery, and it can absorb large amounts of hydrogen gas.

## Silver (Ag), element 47
Silver has the chemical symbol Ag and atomic number 47. It is a transition metal. It is the best electrical and thermal conductor of all metals and has long been used for coins, jewellery and mirrors.

## Cadmium (Cd), element 48
Cadmium has the chemical symbol Cd and atomic number 48. It is a transition metal. It is toxic and its use is restricted, but it has been used in rechargeable batteries and bright pigments.

## Indium (In), element 49
Indium has the chemical symbol In and atomic number 49. It is a post-transition metal. A transparent conducting oxide of indium is used in touchscreens and flat displays, and the metal is soft enough to mark paper.

## Tin (Sn), element 50
Tin has the chemical symbol Sn and atomic number 50. It is a post-transition metal. With copper it forms bronze, and it is used to coat steel cans and in solder.

## Antimony (Sb), element 51
Antimony has the chemical symbol Sb and atomic number 51. It is a metalloid. It is used in flame retardants and in some alloys, and its compounds were once used as cosmetics.

## Tellurium (Te), element 52
Tellurium has the chemical symbol Te and atomic number 52. It is a metalloid. It is used in solar cells and in alloys that are easy to machine.

## Iodine (I), element 53
Iodine has the chemical symbol I and atomic number 53. It is a halogen. It is needed by the thyroid gland, is added to table salt in many countries, and gives off a violet vapour when heated.

## Xenon (Xe), element 54
Xenon has the chemical symbol Xe and atomic number 54. It is a noble gas. It is used in very bright car headlamps and in some spacecraft ion thrusters, and it can act as an anaesthetic.

## Caesium (Cs), element 55
Caesium has the chemical symbol Cs and atomic number 55. It is an alkali metal. Its atomic vibrations define the length of the second in atomic clocks, and it is the most reactive stable metal.

## Barium (Ba), element 56
Barium has the chemical symbol Ba and atomic number 56. It is an alkaline earth metal. Its sulphate is swallowed as a contrast agent for X-ray images of the gut, and its compounds colour fireworks green.

## Lanthanum (La), element 57
Lanthanum has the chemical symbol La and atomic number 57. It is a lanthanide. It is used in camera lenses, in catalysts and in the electrodes of some rechargeable batteries.

## Cerium (Ce), element 58
Cerium has the chemical symbol Ce and atomic number 58. It is a lanthanide and the most abundant rare-earth element. It is used in lighter flints, in self-cleaning ovens and in polishing powders.

## Praseodymium (Pr), element 59
Praseodymium has the chemical symbol Pr and atomic number 59. It is a lanthanide. It is used in very strong magnets and to give glass a yellow-green colour.

## Neodymium (Nd), element 60
Neodymium has the chemical symbol Nd and atomic number 60. It is a lanthanide. Neodymium magnets are among the strongest permanent magnets known and are used in headphones, motors and hard drives.

## Promethium (Pm), element 61
Promethium has the chemical symbol Pm and atomic number 61. It is a lanthanide and the only lanthanide with no stable isotopes. It is radioactive and has been used in luminous paint and in small nuclear batteries.

## Samarium (Sm), element 62
Samarium has the chemical symbol Sm and atomic number 62. It is a lanthanide. Samarium-cobalt magnets keep their magnetism at high temperatures, and its compounds are used in catalysts.

## Europium (Eu), element 63
Europium has the chemical symbol Eu and atomic number 63. It is a lanthanide. Its compounds glow red and blue, and europium was used as the red phosphor in colour television screens.

## Gadolinium (Gd), element 64
Gadolinium has the chemical symbol Gd and atomic number 64. It is a lanthanide. Its compounds are used as contrast agents in MRI scans, and it absorbs neutrons, which suits it to nuclear control rods.

## Terbium (Tb), element 65
Terbium has the chemical symbol Tb and atomic number 65. It is a lanthanide. It is used in green phosphors for displays and in magnets that work at high temperatures.

## Dysprosium (Dy), element 66
Dysprosium has the chemical symbol Dy and atomic number 66. It is a lanthanide. It is added to strong magnets so they keep working at high temperatures, and it absorbs neutrons in reactor control rods.

## Holmium (Ho), element 67
Holmium has the chemical symbol Ho and atomic number 67. It is a lanthanide. It has the highest magnetic moment of any element, and holmium lasers are used in medicine and eye surgery.

## Erbium (Er), element 68
Erbium has the chemical symbol Er and atomic number 68. It is a lanthanide. Erbium-doped fibres amplify light in long-distance optical communication cables.

## Thulium (Tm), element 69
Thulium has the chemical symbol Tm and atomic number 69. It is a lanthanide. One of its radioactive forms is used in small portable X-ray machines, and it is among the rarest of the naturally occurring lanthanides.

## Ytterbium (Yb), element 70
Ytterbium has the chemical symbol Yb and atomic number 70. It is a lanthanide. It is used in some atomic clocks and in lasers, and it improves the strength of stainless steel.

## Lutetium (Lu), element 71
Lutetium has the chemical symbol Lu and atomic number 71. It is a lanthanide and the heaviest of them. Its compounds are used in detectors for PET medical scanners and as catalysts in oil refining.

## Hafnium (Hf), element 72
Hafnium has the chemical symbol Hf and atomic number 72. It is a transition metal. It absorbs neutrons well and is used in nuclear control rods, and its oxide is used in modern computer chips.

## Tantalum (Ta), element 73
Tantalum has the chemical symbol Ta and atomic number 73. It is a transition metal. Tantalum capacitors store charge in a tiny space, which makes them common in phones and laptops, and the metal resists corrosion.

## Tungsten (W), element 74
Tungsten has the chemical symbol W and atomic number 74. It is a transition metal. It has the highest melting point of any metal, which made it the filament in traditional light bulbs, and it is used in cutting tools.

## Rhenium (Re), element 75
Rhenium has the chemical symbol Re and atomic number 75. It is a transition metal. It is one of the rarest elements in the Earth's crust and is used in alloys for jet engine turbine blades.

## Osmium (Os), element 76
Osmium has the chemical symbol Os and atomic number 76. It is a transition metal. It is the densest naturally occurring element and a hard platinum-group metal used in fountain pen nibs and electrical contacts.

## Iridium (Ir), element 77
Iridium has the chemical symbol Ir and atomic number 77. It is a transition metal. It is extremely resistant to corrosion and is used in spark plugs, and a layer of iridium in rocks marks the impact that ended the age of the dinosaurs.

## Platinum (Pt), element 78
Platinum has the chemical symbol Pt and atomic number 78. It is a transition metal. It is a dense, unreactive metal used in jewellery, in laboratory equipment and as a catalyst in catalytic converters.

## Gold (Au), element 79
Gold has the chemical symbol Au and atomic number 79. It is a transition metal. It is prized for jewellery and does not tarnish, and it conducts electricity so well that thin gold layers protect electronic contacts.

## Mercury (Hg), element 80
Mercury has the chemical symbol Hg and atomic number 80. It is a transition metal and the only metal that is liquid at room temperature. It was once used in thermometers, but it is toxic and is now largely avoided.

## Thallium (Tl), element 81
Thallium has the chemical symbol Tl and atomic number 81. It is a post-transition metal. It is highly toxic and was once used as rat poison; it is now used mainly in special optical glass and electronics.

## Lead (Pb), element 82
Lead has the chemical symbol Pb and atomic number 82. It is a post-transition metal. It is dense, soft and toxic, and it is used in car batteries and radiation shielding, with its use restricted because of its health effects.

## Bismuth (Bi), element 83
Bismuth has the chemical symbol Bi and atomic number 83. It is a post-transition metal. It forms striking rainbow-coloured crystals and is used in some stomach medicines and in low-melting alloys.

## Polonium (Po), element 84
Polonium has the chemical symbol Po and atomic number 84. It is a post-transition metal. It is strongly radioactive and was discovered by Marie Curie, who named it after her homeland, Poland.

## Astatine (At), element 85
Astatine has the chemical symbol At and atomic number 85. It is a halogen. It is radioactive and extremely rare, with only tiny traces found in nature at any time.

## Radon (Rn), element 86
Radon has the chemical symbol Rn and atomic number 86. It is a noble gas. It is radioactive and forms naturally from the decay of uranium in rocks, so it can collect in basements.

## Francium (Fr), element 87
Francium has the chemical symbol Fr and atomic number 87. It is an alkali metal. It is extremely radioactive and exists in nature only in tiny traces.

## Radium (Ra), element 88
Radium has the chemical symbol Ra and atomic number 88. It is an alkaline earth metal. It glows faintly because of its radioactivity, and it was discovered by Marie and Pierre Curie; it was once used on watch dials.

## Actinium (Ac), element 89
Actinium has the chemical symbol Ac and atomic number 89. It is an actinide. It is radioactive and glows pale blue in the dark, and some of its radioactive forms are being studied for cancer treatment.

## Thorium (Th), element 90
Thorium has the chemical symbol Th and atomic number 90. It is an actinide. It is a weakly radioactive metal found in rocks and sand, and it has been investigated as a nuclear fuel.

## Protactinium (Pa), element 91
Protactinium has the chemical symbol Pa and atomic number 91. It is an actinide. It is very rare and strongly radioactive, and it is found in tiny amounts in uranium ores.

## Uranium (U), element 92
Uranium has the chemical symbol U and atomic number 92. It is an actinide. It is the heaviest element found in significant amounts in nature and the main fuel of nuclear power reactors.

## Neptunium (Np), element 93
Neptunium has the chemical symbol Np and atomic number 93. It is an actinide. It was the first element made beyond uranium, produced artificially, and it is radioactive.

## Plutonium (Pu), element 94
Plutonium has the chemical symbol Pu and atomic number 94. It is an actinide. It is radioactive and is used in nuclear weapons and in some nuclear reactors and space power supplies.

## Americium (Am), element 95
Americium has the chemical symbol Am and atomic number 95. It is an actinide. A tiny amount of it is used in many smoke detectors, where its radiation helps detect smoke particles.

## Curium (Cm), element 96
Curium has the chemical symbol Cm and atomic number 96. It is an actinide. It is radioactive and named after Marie and Pierre Curie, and it has been used as an energy source in space probes.

## Berkelium (Bk), element 97
Berkelium has the chemical symbol Bk and atomic number 97. It is an actinide. It is radioactive and was first made at the University of California in Berkeley, after which it is named.

## Californium (Cf), element 98
Californium has the chemical symbol Cf and atomic number 98. It is an actinide. It is radioactive and a strong source of neutrons, used to start up nuclear reactors and in some cancer treatments.

## Einsteinium (Es), element 99
Einsteinium has the chemical symbol Es and atomic number 99. It is an actinide. It is radioactive and named after Albert Einstein, and it was discovered in the debris of the first hydrogen bomb test.

## Fermium (Fm), element 100
Fermium has the chemical symbol Fm and atomic number 100. It is an actinide. It is radioactive and named after Enrico Fermi, and it was also first found in fallout from a nuclear test.

## Mendelevium (Md), element 101
Mendelevium has the chemical symbol Md and atomic number 101. It is an actinide. It is radioactive and named after Dmitri Mendeleev, the creator of the periodic table.

## Nobelium (No), element 102
Nobelium has the chemical symbol No and atomic number 102. It is an actinide. It is radioactive and named after Alfred Nobel, the inventor of dynamite and founder of the Nobel Prizes.

## Lawrencium (Lr), element 103
Lawrencium has the chemical symbol Lr and atomic number 103. It is an actinide. It is radioactive and named after Ernest Lawrence, the inventor of the cyclotron particle accelerator.

## Rutherfordium (Rf), element 104
Rutherfordium has the chemical symbol Rf and atomic number 104. It is a transition metal. It is a highly radioactive element made artificially and named after Ernest Rutherford, who discovered the atomic nucleus.

## Dubnium (Db), element 105
Dubnium has the chemical symbol Db and atomic number 105. It is a transition metal. It is a radioactive element made artificially and named after the town of Dubna in Russia, a centre of nuclear research.

## Seaborgium (Sg), element 106
Seaborgium has the chemical symbol Sg and atomic number 106. It is a transition metal. It is a radioactive element made artificially and named after Glenn Seaborg, who codiscovered many heavy elements.

## Bohrium (Bh), element 107
Bohrium has the chemical symbol Bh and atomic number 107. It is a transition metal. It is a radioactive element made artificially and named after Niels Bohr, a founder of quantum theory.

## Hassium (Hs), element 108
Hassium has the chemical symbol Hs and atomic number 108. It is a transition metal. It is a radioactive element made artificially and named after the German state of Hesse, where it was first produced.

## Meitnerium (Mt), element 109
Meitnerium has the chemical symbol Mt and atomic number 109. It is a transition metal. It is a radioactive element made artificially and named after Lise Meitner, who helped explain nuclear fission.

## Darmstadtium (Ds), element 110
Darmstadtium has the chemical symbol Ds and atomic number 110. It is a transition metal. It is a radioactive element made artificially and named after Darmstadt in Germany, where it was discovered.

## Roentgenium (Rg), element 111
Roentgenium has the chemical symbol Rg and atomic number 111. It is a transition metal. It is a radioactive element made artificially and named after Wilhelm Roentgen, who discovered X-rays.

## Copernicium (Cn), element 112
Copernicium has the chemical symbol Cn and atomic number 112. It is a transition metal. It is a radioactive element made artificially and named after Nicolaus Copernicus, who placed the Sun at the centre of the Solar System.

## Nihonium (Nh), element 113
Nihonium has the chemical symbol Nh and atomic number 113. It is a post-transition metal. It is a radioactive element made artificially and named after Japan (Nihon), where it was first produced.

## Flerovium (Fl), element 114
Flerovium has the chemical symbol Fl and atomic number 114. It is a post-transition metal. It is a radioactive element made artificially and named after the Flerov Laboratory in Russia.

## Moscovium (Mc), element 115
Moscovium has the chemical symbol Mc and atomic number 115. It is a post-transition metal. It is a radioactive element made artificially and named after the Moscow region of Russia.

## Livermorium (Lv), element 116
Livermorium has the chemical symbol Lv and atomic number 116. It is a post-transition metal. It is a radioactive element made artificially and named after the Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory in California.

## Tennessine (Ts), element 117
Tennessine has the chemical symbol Ts and atomic number 117. It is a halogen. It is a radioactive element made artificially and named after the state of Tennessee in the United States.

## Oganesson (Og), element 118
Oganesson has the chemical symbol Og and atomic number 118. It is a noble gas and the last element of the standard periodic table. It is a radioactive element made artificially and named after Yuri Oganessian, a pioneer in creating heavy elements.
